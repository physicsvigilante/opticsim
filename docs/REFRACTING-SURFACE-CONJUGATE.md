# Finite object imaging through refracting surfaces

Use `refracting-surface-conjugate` with the problem workflow for a finite object on the incident side of one to four ordered coaxial interfaces. A planar face models apparent depth, two curved faces model a thick lens, and two separated thick lenses can use all four faces. The [worked input](../examples/refracting-surface-conjugate.json) specifies the second lens and the air gap through its two pole positions.

Declare a common length unit (`mm`, `cm`, or `m`), the incident absolute index, the object x coordinate and each surface's stable ID, pole x coordinate, signed radius and transmitted absolute index. Positive radius places the center downstream. Use the string `"infinity"` for a plane. Surface order is the physical transmission order along +x. The medium after a surface is incident on the next one. All indices lie in [1,4]. Positions have magnitude at most 10000 mm. The object-to-first-pole and inter-pole distances are at least 1 mm. Finite radius magnitudes span 1–10000 mm.

Using reduced slope q = n dy/dx, a homogeneous interval adds (d/n)q to the ray height. A curved interface subtracts [(n₂−n₁)/R]y from q. For the second column (B,D) of the composed object-to-final-pole matrix, the signed conjugate distance from that pole is −n₂B/D. The unit determinant gives transverse magnification 1/D. An explicit object height also yields a signed image height. Positive distance is a real conjugate and negative distance a virtual one. A zero B locates the image at the final pole. At exact D = 0, the emerging pencil is collimated and finite distance and magnification are withheld.

Calculations use exact fractions for the represented inputs. Nonzero near-cancellations at relative scale 1e−10, distances beyond 10⁹ mm, and unrepresentable intermediate conversions withhold the result. The answer assumes transmitted first-order optics. Finite apertures, aberration and detector evidence belong to a separately specified native scene. The [three frozen synthetic controls](../evaluation/thick-lens-finite-conjugate-study-v1/README.md) document the native comparison for their sampled heights and slopes.
