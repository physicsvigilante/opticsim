# Phase-dependent axial mirror conjugates

Prescribe the mean distance and amplitude of a cosine axial position, both divided by the positive concave mirror focal length. The phase τ is the fraction t/T of one cycle. The stationary mirror calculation uses the instantaneous paraxial conjugate at each phase. It accepts a trajectory that keeps the real object in front of the mirror throughout the cycle.

For normalized object distance U = mean + amplitude cos(2πτ), the normalized image distance is U/(U−1) and the signed lateral magnification is −1/(U−1). A focal phase at U = 1 has collimated output. Coincidence is the separate condition U = 2. Both sets are returned with their phase classification. An exact focal phase carries no finite distance or magnification. A numerically unresolved event or sample leaves the overall result partially indeterminate while the resolved phases and law remain available.

In A08, x is measured outward from the centre of curvature, so U = 2 + cos(2πτ). Coincidence occurs at the centre for τ = 1/4 and 3/4. At τ = 1/2 there is no finite lateral magnification, and nearby phases tend to negative unbounded magnification in the model. The three native controls are independent static scenes. The focal scene checks finite residual slopes without identifying a finite focus.
