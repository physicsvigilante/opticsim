# Focus and object placement for a refracting face and rear reflector

Light enters at the front pole, travels through homogeneous material, reflects at the rear, and exits through the same front face. In the original incident coordinate system a positive radius places the centre downstream of its pole. On return, reported distances increase from the front pole toward the incident object.

Supply the exterior and interior absolute indices (both in [1,4]) and either of two geometries. For `signed-poles`, declare the signed radius of each face and their axial separation. Use `"infinity"` for a plane. Zero separation declares an analytical thin element. Positive separation and finite radius magnitudes span 1–10000 mm. For `equiconvex-thin-from-focal-length`, give the positive focal length of the uncoated lens in the stated exterior. The common radius magnitude is 2f(n−n₀)/n₀. The lengths use one explicit unit throughout.

* `refracting-mirror-return-focus` gives the power-equivalent focal length and the signed intercept of collimated input relative to the front pole. For separated faces the two distances can differ. Zero power returns an explicit afocal state.
* `refracting-mirror-self-conjugate` gives all distinct positive distances for which a real incident object forms its paraxial image at the same position. A front-pole location and virtual objects fall outside this request.

In travel coordinates (y,q=n dy/ds), each refraction changes q by −(n−n₀)y/R₁. Each translation changes y by dq/n. Reflection changes q by +2ny/R₂ in the returning travel frame. For the resulting matrix with entries A,B,C,D, the power-equivalent focal length is −n₀/C and the front-pole focus is −n₀A/C. Because this round trip is symmetric, A=D and A²−BC=1. Positive self-conjugate distances satisfy B+2A(s/n₀)+C(s/n₀)²=0, including its linear limit at C=0. Exact represented arithmetic distinguishes a zero term from near cancellation, with unresolved relative cancellations at 1e−10 and output distances beyond 10⁹ mm withheld.

Lens Problems 004 and 006 motivate the [focus example](../examples/refracting-mirror-return-focus.json) and [coincidence example](../examples/refracting-mirror-self-conjugate.json). Each explicitly assumes a unit-index exterior. Problem 004 additionally uses the thin-element approximation. Under these assumptions the first acts like a concave mirror of focal length 15 cm, and the second returns an image to a real object 12.5 cm in front. The [pinned native experiment](../evaluation/refracting-face-mirror-return-study-v1/README.md) provides separate evidence at a specified finite thickness.
