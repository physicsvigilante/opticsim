# Angular gain and virtual conjugacy of a separated thin-lens pair

The two queries use a real object, two positive thin lenses and an on-axis eye plane in unit-index air. Give both focal lengths and IDs, the lens-plane separation, the object distance from the objective and the eye distance from the eyepiece. The unaided comparison uses the same object and eye planes for each arrangement. All input lengths are explicit and lie between 1 and 10000 mm.

From the object plane to the eye plane, let the ray transfer matrix have entries A, B, C and D. A small ray from object height h that crosses the optical axis at the eye has initial slope −Ah/B. Its slope at the eye is −h/B because AD−BC=1. The unaided slope at the same eye is −h/(u+d+e), where u is the object distance, d the lens separation and e the eye offset. The signed angular gain is (u+d+e)/B. The signed virtual distance from the eye is −B/D.

The swap query keeps u and e fixed and moves the eyepiece plane so the lenses exchange order while preserving signed angular gain. After exchange B depends linearly on d, so equating B/(u+d+e) gives a single candidate. Both arrangements must produce a finite virtual image relative to the eyepiece. Singular conjugates and ill-conditioned ratios withhold the result. The output also gives each virtual distance so the required viewing condition is explicit.

The original A38 gives the lens focal lengths and their separation. The object distance, eye offset, unaided comparison and distances retained during exchange are unspecified. A relaxed microscope with a real intermediate image cannot use the stated 0.2 m separation for those focal lengths. The separate explicit control uses u=40 mm and e=50 mm. Its gain is 174/137 and the swapped separation is 26000/101 mm. These are conditional results for the numeric control.
