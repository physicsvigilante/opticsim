# Collimated focus of two transversely offset lenses

Give the two parallel thin lenses distinct IDs and specify their signed focal-length ratio q = f₂/f₁ and axial separation k = gap/f₁. The first lens has positive focal length. The origin is its centre, positive x follows propagation, and the signed transverse shift d runs from its axis to the second lens axis. The model assumes paraxial collimated illumination along x and applies to the bundle that encounters both lenses. The physical apertures and the number of rays in that bundle require a separate native scene.

In units of f₁, the transfer matrix gives the exit height sensitivity A and slope sensitivity C to input height. At z = −A/C from lens two the input-height dependence cancels. The ray entering through the first lens centre has height zero at lens two and leaves at slope d/f₂, giving x/f₁ = k + z and y = (z/q)d. These outputs work without supplying either scale. A negative z describes an upstream extrapolation. For an exactly afocal pair the query returns the common slope coefficient and withholds finite coordinates. Near cancellation or excessively large normalized coordinates withhold a numerical result.

For A35, k = 3 and q = 1 give x = 5f and y = 2d. The existing v20 example measures the finite realization f = 200 mm and d = 2 mm. Its two detector sections yield outgoing rays that extrapolate to (1000, 4) mm. The test-only controls also cover upstream extrapolation, collimated output and concurrence in the second lens plane.
